# Founding board

Seats 7 to 9 are for strong directors who do not map cleanly onto one of the five function seats, and who take the board from six to nine.

Notarium is recruiting up to three founding directors for the at-large seats. Each seat is defined by what you bring, not by a slot you were hired to fill.

## What an at-large seat is

The board has five core seats defined by function: Legal / Governance, Finance / Treasurer, Patient / Community, Technical / Security, and Fundraising / Development. The three at-large seats exist for strong directors who do not map cleanly onto one of those five. They bring the board from its six-seat floor to its nine-seat target without forcing every good candidate into a predefined lane.

## Who I am looking for

Community members, volunteers, and contributors who strengthen the board through general judgment, lived experience, community standing, or a specialized background the core seats do not cover. Backgrounds that make strong at-large candidates include:

- Medical or clinical practice (physician, PT or OT, nurse, or clinical researcher) with a chronic-illness or ADA-accommodation practice
- Academic or research work in patient-centered outcomes, health informatics, or bioethics
- A second or third patient-community perspective, a different condition, demographic, or accommodation experience than the Patient / Community seat
- Open-source community leadership, technical writing, communications, or public education
- Any other lived or professional experience that makes the board wiser about the people Notarium serves

You do not need to arrive with a committee assignment. At-large directors are matched to committee work that fits their strengths once seated.

## Why these seats matter

- They take the board from six to nine, which is where quorum resilience, staggered-term stability, and functional committees (Audit, Finance, Governance, Executive) all get easier to sustain.
- They break tie votes. A six-seat board can still deadlock three to three; odd seats above six remove routine ties.
- They keep the board from being locked into only five predefined viewpoints as the org and its community grow.
- Because at-large directors are recruited from outside the founder's professional circles, they raise the share of directors independent of the founder, a point IRS Form 1023 asks about directly and that grantmakers look for.

## What you would do

- Bring an independent perspective to board votes and to the annual roadmap review
- Serve on, and over time possibly chair, a committee that fits your background
- Represent the board in community, patient, or professional settings when interest and capacity allow
- Contribute to grant applications where your perspective or credibility strengthens the case
- Advise on decisions that touch your area of experience

## Independence

These are the seats where the founder-independence line is easiest to hold, and where I most want to hold it. At-large directors should not be friends or professional connections of mine. If a community connection surfaces someone qualified whom I already know, we route the vetting through a different director so the independence requirement stands.

## Time commitment

The base of four hours per month. No committee chair expectation unless you want it.

## What you get

- A vote on how a patient-facing FOSS health nonprofit gets built and governed
- Public credit as a founding director
- A board seat defined by what you bring, not by a slot you were hired to fill

## How to express interest

Email me at sophia@notarium.health and say an at-large seat looks like a fit. I will send a forty-five minute intro call invitation and answer any questions before either of us commits. A call is not a commitment to serve. If it feels right, we set a start date after the 501(c)(3) formation completes.

Referrals are especially welcome for these seats. Their whole purpose is reach beyond the founder's network.

## About Notarium

Notarium is a private, local-first Android app for people managing chronic illness or disability while navigating workplace leave and accommodations. It helps users log symptoms, track medications, record flare-ups, and preserve documentation that holds up under HR review or an EEOC charge. Everything stays on the user's device by default, encrypted at the file system, at the database, and per field for the most sensitive columns. Any upstream sync is opt-in per data category, never global. The source is public under AGPL-3.0.
