# Code of Conduct

Notarium uses the Contributor Covenant, version 2.1, unmodified except for the enforcement contact, followed by health-community addenda that carry the same weight and are enforced the same way.

The authoritative copy is [CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](https://github.com/notariumhealth/notarium/blob/main/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) in the source repository. This page carries the same text so it can be read and linked without cloning anything. Conduct on the project's own public accounts is a separate matter, covered on the [account conduct](/account-conduct) page.

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Our standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment for our community include:

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
- Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes, and learning from the experience
- Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the overall community

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

- The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without their explicit permission
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Enforcement responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned to this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and also applies when an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces. Examples of representing our community include using an official email address, posting via an official social media account, or acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event.

The project's own public accounts carry further commitments, published on the [account conduct](/account-conduct) page.

## Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be reported to the community leaders responsible for enforcement at [conduct@notarium.health](mailto:conduct@notarium.health). All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly.

All community leaders are obligated to respect the privacy and security of the reporter of any incident.

## Enforcement guidelines

Community leaders will follow these Community Impact Guidelines in determining the consequences for any action they deem in violation of this Code of Conduct:

### 1. Correction

**Community Impact**: Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence**: A private, written warning from community leaders, providing clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Community Impact**: A violation through a single incident or series of actions.

**Consequence**: A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time. This includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external channels like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary ban

**Community Impact**: A serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence**: A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period of time. No public or private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, is allowed during this period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent ban

**Community Impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence**: A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the community.

## Health community addenda

Everything above is the Contributor Covenant 2.1, unmodified except for the enforcement contact address. Everything below is specific to Notarium. It carries the same weight and is enforced the same way.

Notarium is used by people managing chronic illness and disability, often while navigating a workplace accommodation process. Project spaces are public. An employer, an insurer, or opposing counsel can read them. These rules exist because of that.

### Health disclosure is voluntary

Nobody has to disclose a diagnosis, a symptom, a medication, or a disability status to participate here. That includes filing a bug, requesting a feature, or arguing for a design change.

Do not ask a person to justify a request by explaining their medical situation. If a feature request needs clarification, ask about the behavior of the software, not about the body of the person asking.

Maintainers will not require reproduction steps that force a reporter to reveal health information. If reproducing a bug appears to need that, the maintainer will find another way or accept a partial report.

### Health disclosures stay where they were made

If somebody shares health information in an issue, a pull request, a forum thread, or a private message, that information stays there.

Do not screenshot it. Do not quote it into social media. Do not repeat it in a conference talk, a blog post, or a grant application. Do not paraphrase it as "a user told us" in any public channel. This holds even when the original space is public and even when the person's handle is removed. Aggregated, non-identifying statements about usage are fine. Individual stories are not, without that person's explicit permission for that specific use.

### No medical or legal advice

Do not give medical advice in project spaces. Do not tell somebody what their symptoms mean, whether a medication interaction is safe, or whether they should see a doctor.

Do not give legal advice in project spaces. Do not tell somebody whether they have a valid ADA claim, whether their employer broke the law, or what they should file and when.

Notarium helps people document their own situation so they can take it to somebody qualified. Pointing at general public resources is fine. Telling somebody what to do about their own case is not, and it exposes them and the project to real harm.

### Assume a hostile reader

Write as though the person on the other side of an accommodation dispute is reading the thread, because they might be.

Do not encourage anyone to post details of an ongoing legal or HR matter in a public space. If a discussion starts heading that way, say so and move it.

### Enforcement of these addenda

Same contact, same process, same consequences ladder as above: [conduct@notarium.health](mailto:conduct@notarium.health).

Violations of the health addenda are treated at least as seriously as violations of the base Code of Conduct. Publishing somebody's health information without permission is grounds for a permanent ban on a first offense.
